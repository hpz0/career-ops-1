## Procurement policy — what to expect & how to answer

### "What goes in the policy?"

A good policy is short on philosophy, long on **operating rules**:

| Section | What it covers |
|---------|----------------|
| **Scope & authority** | Who procurement is; what spend it owns; when a PO is required |
| **Delegation of authority** | Approval matrix by $ threshold, category, and risk |
| **Sourcing rules** | Competitive bid thresholds, sole-source justification, emergency buys |
| **Vendor onboarding** | NDA, W-9/insurance, bank details, contract before first PO, master data standards |
| **Contract & payment** | No work without PO/contract; match-to-PO; prohibited purchases |
| **Conflicts & ethics** | Gifts, related parties, confidentiality |
| **Records** | Retention, audit trail, system of record (ERP/P2P) |

*"At Plenty I authored the policy with input from Finance, Legal, and IT so it wasn't a procurement document sitting on a shelf — it was the control framework Finance could point to in an audit."*

### "How do you roll it out so people actually follow it?"

1. **Executive sponsor** — VP Finance (or CFO) sends the "why now" message; procurement owns the "how."
2. **Make compliance the easy path** — catalogs, preferred suppliers, clear thresholds, fast turnaround SLAs.
3. **Train by persona** — requesters vs approvers vs AP; 30-minute sessions + office hours, not a 40-page read.
4. **Measure exceptions** — weekly maverick spend report to Finance; name categories/sites, not people (at first).
5. **Graduated enforcement** — coach → escalate → block only for high-risk (new vendor, no PO, over threshold).
6. **Quarterly refresh** — policy is v1.0; tighten based on what breaks in the first 90 days.

*"Adoption is the hard part. Plenty proved that — 99% on contract came from enforcement in the system and partnership with AP, not from sending a PDF."*

### "How do you manage ongoing compliance?"

- **System controls** — can't pay without PO where required; approval routing enforced in ERP/P2P
- **Exception dashboards** — off-contract spend, late approvals, invoice without receipt
- **Vendor master governance** — one front door; no duplicate vendors across entities post-merger
- **Spot audits** — sample PO files, bid documentation, contract linkage
- **Feedback loop** — if a rule is ignored 80% of the time, fix the process or the rule

### What SOX means for procurement (plain English)

SOX is about **internal controls over financial reporting**. For procurement, that translates to:

| Control theme | What it means in practice |
|---------------|---------------------------|
| **Segregation of duties** | Person who requests ≠ sole approver ≠ receives goods ≠ processes payment |
| **Authorization** | Documented approval authority; no "after the fact" POs for material spend |
| **Completeness & accuracy** | PO matches contract/quote; invoice matches PO and receipt (**three-way match**) |
| **Vendor master integrity** | New vendors vetted; bank changes verified; no phantom suppliers |
| **Audit trail** | Every change logged in ERP/P2P — who approved what, when |
| **Period close** | Accruals for goods received not invoiced (GRNI); no surprise AP at month-end |

*"Even at a private company, I design P2P the way a SOX auditor would ask about it — because that's what keeps Finance out of fire drills at close."*

### If they ask: "Have you lived SOX?"

**Honest frame:** Plenty was private/high-growth — I built **audit-ready controls** (policy, approvals, system enforcement, Finance/Legal sign-off) that map to SOX expectations. At Comcast, enterprise procurement operates in a **heavily controlled** environment on multi-billion categories. You design for the control standard, whether or not the company is public today.

## AI in procurement — talking points

*They may ask how you'd use AI in a greenfield function. Keep it practical — VP Finance wants ROI and risk management, not hype.*

### Where AI helps (start here)

- **Spend analytics** — classify maverick spend, find duplicate vendors across 17 sites / post-merger entities
- **Contract intelligence** — extract renewal dates, payment terms, auto-renew clauses from CLM/PDF backlog
- **Invoice coding** — suggest GL/cost center; flag anomalies before AP posts
- **Supplier research** — market pricing benchmarks, risk signals (sanctions, financial distress)
- **RFP support** — summarize vendor responses; **human decides**, AI accelerates read-in

### Where to be careful

- **No AI approving spend** — human approval stays in the workflow; AI informs
- **No unsourced commitments** — don't let AI negotiate or send POs without review
- **Data hygiene first** — AI on bad vendor master = garbage in, garbage out
- **Start narrow** — one use case (e.g., spend classification report for Finance) before "AI transformation"

### One-liner

> "I'd use AI to give  **visibility faster** — spend mapping, exception detection, contract metadata — not to replace approvals. The control environment comes first; AI accelerates the diagnosis and the reporting."
