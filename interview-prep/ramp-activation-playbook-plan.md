# If I Got the Job — Ramp Procurement Product Activation Plan

**Role:** Senior Manager, Procurement Product Activation  
**Segment:** Mid-market (≈100–999 employees, ~$50–100M revenue)  
**Job:** Make Ramp Procurement go-lives excellent, repeatable, and fast — by building the **system** (playbook + people + handoffs), not by heroically saving every account yourself.

Use this in HM / later rounds. Recruiter only needs the **60-sec version** at the bottom of the cheatsheet.

---

## North star (how I’d define winning)

| Horizon | What “good” looks like |
|---------|-------------------------|
| **90 days** | v1 playbook live; hiring bar clear; I’m on escalation calls without day-to-day babysitting; Sales/AM/CSM know when to pull Activation |
| **6 months** | Specialists ramping to independence; activation is the default path for procurement deals; Product gets structured feedback weekly; I’m the internal procurement authority people call first |
| **12 months** | Motion survives growth + new product features; clear path to clone the specialist model in other product areas |

**Principle I won’t compromise:** Platform alone doesn’t create adoption. **Policy + approvals + ERP reality + change management** have to move with the product. That’s the Coupa lesson — it becomes the spine of the playbook.

## First 90 days

### Days 1–30 — Listen, map, earn the room
**Goal:** Understand current reality before writing doctrine.

1. **Shadow** live mid-market activations end-to-end (discovery → go-live). Take notes on where deals stall: intake design, approval politics, NetSuite/ERP, legal/CLM, training gaps, oversold scope.
2. **Interview** AEs, AMs, CSMs, Product, and any existing specialists — same questions every time: *What does a clean handoff look like? Where do you lose time? What do customers surprise us with?*
3. **Catalog failure modes** into a simple taxonomy:
   - Product gap  
   - Bad discovery / wrong config  
   - Change-management / training miss  
   - Handoff / resourcing miss  
   - Customer unreadiness (no owner, no policy, no ERP access)
4. **Join 2–3 hard customer calls** myself (escalations or complex Controllers) — prove procurement fluency early; don’t hide behind “I’m just the manager.”
5. **Draft hiring scorecard** before hiring volume ramps (see Team Build below).

**Exit criteria for day 30:** One-page “current state” map + ranked top 5 systemic blockers + agreement with HM on what v1 playbook must cover.

### Days 31–60 — Write v1 playbook + install the operating rhythm
**Goal:** Specialists can execute without inventing the job every time.

1. Ship **Playbook v1** (sections below) — ugly and usable beats perfect and unread.
2. Stand up **weekly rhythms**:
   - Specialist 1:1s (pipeline, stuck deals, coaching moments)
   - Activation standup / deal review (red/yellow/green by stage)
   - Product feedback hour (patterns → tickets, not anecdotes)
3. Align **Sales → Activation → CSM handoff** standards with peer leaders (written: what “sales-assisted ready” means; what Activation owns vs CSM).
4. Run one **calibration**: same discovery call reviewed by me + specialists against the playbook checklist — align on “excellent.”
5. Start hiring against the scorecard (or backfill) with HM.

**Exit criteria for day 60:** Playbook v1 adopted in live deals; handoff doc signed off by Sales/AM/CSM leads; at least one specialist visibly improving from coaching.

### Days 61–90 — Make it stick + scale readiness
**Goal:** System runs when I’m not in the room.

1. Tighten playbook from real deal friction (v1.1) — kill steps that don’t change outcomes.
2. Own escalations independently; use them as **teaching artifacts** (write the postmortem into the playbook).
3. Publish a **simple dashboard** everyone trusts: time-to-go-live, % on-time go-live, stage aging, reopen/escalation rate, specialist load.
4. Present to GTM/Product: “Here’s what mid-market procurement activation needs next” — seat at the table earned with patterns, not opinions.
5. 90-day review with HM: what’s working, hire plan, playbook gaps, my own development.

**Exit criteria for day 90:** Matches JD’s 6-month direction early — reputation as procurement authority starting; playbook + coaching loop real.

## The Playbook I’d Write

Not a novel. A **field manual** specialists open on Monday morning. Four stages + operating appendices.

### Stage 0 — Intake / handoff (before customer kickoff)
**Owner:** AE/AM + Activation  
**Excellent looks like:**
- Deal scope clear: Procurement only vs Procurement + Bill Pay (or other modules)
- Buyer map: economic buyer (often CFO/Controller), day-to-day owner, IT/ERP contact, legal if CLM-sensitive
- Current-state sketch from sales notes: how they buy today (email? Slack? legacy P2P?), ERP (NetSuite / QBO / other), approval pain
- Red flags flagged pre-kickoff: no executive sponsor, “we’ll figure process later,” custom ERP nightmares, legal bottleneck

**Specialist checklist:** Accept/reject readiness; schedule kickoff within X days; pull right internal partners.

### Stage 1 — Discovery (redesign how they buy)
**Owner:** Specialist with Controller / finance ops  
**Excellent looks like:**
- Mapped **intake → approve → order/pay → reconcile** as they do it *today*
- Named exception paths (who bypasses policy, emergency buys, vendor onboarding)
- Success metrics agreed in their language: cycle time, on-contract %, AP touch time, visibility — not “use Ramp more”
- Future-state on Ramp designed *with* them, not presented as a demo replay
- Explicit “won’t do in v1” list (scope control — prevents Sales oversell from becoming Activation failure)

**Manager coaching focus:** Are they asking about process and politics, or only clicking product?

### Stage 2 — Configuration
**Owner:** Specialist (+ solutions/tech as needed)  
**Excellent looks like:**
- Approvals mirror real authority (not org-chart fantasy)
- ERP / accounting sync path confirmed early; blockers escalated same week
- Policies/thresholds encoded so employees can buy without tribal knowledge
- Test transactions in a sandbox mindset before “go live” theater
- Config decisions documented so CSM inherits context

**Manager coaching focus:** Speed without silent landmines — wrong approval design creates permanent shadow process.

### Stage 3 — Customer education
**Owner:** Specialist  
**Excellent looks like:**
- Train **requesters + approvers + AP/finance**, not only the sponsor
- Role-based enablement (3 audiences, short sessions)
- “Day in the life” practice: submit → approve → exception
- Champions identified inside the customer (someone who will nag peers when you’re gone)

**Manager coaching focus:** Adoption is a people problem wearing a software costume.

### Stage 4 — Go-live + handoff to CSM
**Owner:** Specialist → CSM  
**Excellent looks like:**
- Go-live criteria checklist (green/yellow/red) — no “vibes-based” launch
- Hypercare window with known escalation path
- Clean written handoff: open risks, config quirks, political landmines, expansion hints
- Customer can run a week without the specialist as human middleware

**Manager coaching focus:** Handoffs that CSM trusts = Activation’s brand inside Ramp.

### Appendices (the “system” layer)

| Appendix | Purpose |
|----------|---------|
| **A. Escalation taxonomy** | Product vs discovery vs training vs handoff vs customer unreadiness — who owns the fix |
| **B. Product feedback template** | Pattern → frequency → revenue/time impact → proposed fix (not “customer asked for X”) |
| **C. Deal desk / scope rules** | What Activation will / won’t swallow when Sales sold ahead of readiness |
| **D. Specialist quality bar** | Call scorecard: discovery depth, Controller credibility, config judgment, education clarity |
| **E. Mid-market segment notes** | Typical 100–999 company constraints: thin procurement staff, Controller wears many hats, ERP = NetSuite/QBO heavy |
| **F. Vendor onboarding / credit** | How credit is opened (vendor app vs company packet); who signs; don’t casually accept vendor boilerplate terms — see story **S7** |

**How I’d keep it alive:** Every red deal produces one playbook edit or one Product ticket. Playbook that doesn’t change is dead.

### Product feedback seed (from your experience — S7)
Pattern to watch in the field: vendors push **their** credit applications (terms buried inside). Strong Controllers often prefer a **Finance-approved company packet** with the data vendors need — without signing vendor paper. If Ramp automates “fill credit forms,” Activation should push for **term-safe / Controller-controlled** flows, not speed-at-any-risk.

## How I’d Build the Team

### Who I’d hire (scorecard)
Bias to **trainable operators with judgment**, not pure product tour guides.

| Must-have | Nice-to-have | Avoid |
|-----------|--------------|--------|
| Comfort with finance/Controller conversations | Prior procurement, AP, or P2P exposure | Pure script-readers who freeze without a deck |
| Structured discovery (leaves with requirements) | NetSuite / ERP familiarity | Heroes who refuse to write anything down |
| Coaching-receptive / high slope | CS or implementation background | Blame-the-customer default |
| Clear writing | Startup pace | Needs constant escalation for basic judgment |

**Interview loop I’d run:** (1) Controller role-play discovery, (2) “deal is stuck — diagnose” case, (3) write a 1-page handoff from a messy scenario, (4) values/slope chat.

### How I’d ramp a new specialist (first 30 days on team)
Week 1: shadow only + playbook read-through  
Week 2: reverse-shadow — they lead discovery, I debrief  
Week 3: own a clean mid-complexity account with me on speed-dial  
Week 4: independent with structured 1:1; graded against quality bar  

Same motion as S6 (junior buyer → category owner): **scoped ownership → weekly decision coaching → shadow/reverse-shadow → checklist so quality holds when I’m gone.**

### How I’d coach ongoing
- 1:1s are about **judgment**, not status theater (“What did you decide and why?”)
- Review 1 recorded/customer call per specialist per month against the scorecard
- Promote people who make the *next* person faster — not only personal go-live counts
- Public praise for clean handoffs and Product tickets that changed the roadmap

### Culture I’d protect
- AEs/AMs/CSMs come to Activation **before** it’s on fire  
- We tell the truth about scope and readiness (earn trust once; lose it forever if we rubber-stamp bad deals)  
- Procurement authority = credibility with customers **and** internal teams

## Verbal answers (practice these)

### 60–90 sec — “How would you approach the role?”
> I’d treat this as building an operating system, not managing a queue. First thirty days I’d shadow live mid-market activations, map where deals actually stall — discovery, approvals, ERP, training, handoffs — and earn credibility on a few Controller calls myself. Days thirty to sixty I’d ship a v1 playbook covering discovery through go-live, plus written handoff standards with Sales, AM, and CSM, and install a coaching rhythm so specialists move from reactive to judgment calls. By ninety days the playbook should be in live use, escalations should route through a clear taxonomy, and Product should be getting patterns, not anecdotes. I’ve done the buyer-side version of this — Coupa, policy, coaching buyers into ownership — so I’m not guessing what “excellent” looks like for a Controller.

### 45 sec — “What would your playbook include?”
> Four stages: discovery that redesigns how they buy, configuration that matches real approvals and ERP, education for requesters and approvers not just the sponsor, and a go-live checklist with a clean CSM handoff. Plus appendices for escalation types, Product feedback format, and what we won’t accept as ready from Sales. Short, used weekly, updated every time a red deal teaches us something.

### 45 sec — “How would you build the team?”
> Hire for Controller-ready discovery and judgment, then ramp with shadow, reverse-shadow, and scoped ownership — same way I developed a junior buyer into an IT category owner. Weekly 1:1s on decisions, not status. Quality bar on calls. I want people who make the playbook better, not heroes who only win when they’re in the room.
