## Nuclear Energy 101 (expanded)

### Why nuclear exists as a product
1. **Baseload / firm power** — high **capacity factor** (runs near full output most hours of the year).
2. **Clean (operating)** — no CO₂ at the stack while generating.
3. **Energy density** — large output from a compact site vs equivalent continuous renewable + storage footprint.
4. **Long fuel cycles** — many light-water reactors refuel every **~18–24 months**, then return to high output.
5. **Demand + geopolitics** — data centers/AI; competition with China/Russia on reactor deployment pace (company narrative).

### Fission → electrons (keep it simple)
Nuclear **fission** in fuel rods releases heat → heats water → **steam** → **turbine** → **generator** → grid. Control systems (including control rods) manage the reaction rate. Coolant removes heat safely. Stop there unless they invite more.

### PWR vs BWR ([World Nuclear Association](https://world-nuclear.org/information-library/nuclear-power-reactors/overview/nuclear-power-reactors))

| | PWR | BWR |
|---|-----|-----|
| Setup | Primary loop **pressurized** (water stays liquid) → heat exchanger (**steam generator**) → secondary loop makes steam for turbine | Water **boils in the reactor**; steam goes to turbine |
| US relevance | Most common commercial type — **focus of their Interview Guide** | Also used; fewer units |
| Why care | Fleet standardization story usually assumes a chosen design family (PWR-class) | Awareness only |

**PWR 15-sec:** Fuel heats pressurized water that doesn’t boil; heat makes steam in a separate loop; steam spins the turbine.

### FOAK / NOAK / fleet
- **FOAK:** first plant of a design — expensive learning.
- **NOAK:** nth copy — cheaper/faster *if* design and supply chain stay stable.
- **Fleet-scale:** deliberately building a series so learning compounds; suppliers, specs, and construction playbooks stabilize.
- **Vogtle lesson (industry framing):** finish design before construction; mature suppliers; train workforce — otherwise rework dominates. ([DOE Advanced Nuclear Liftoff](https://liftoff.energy.gov/); Reuters coverage of Vogtle challenges)

### SMR vs large GW plants
- **SMR:** smaller modular designs, often factory-built modules; different commercial model.
- **TNC:** public emphasis on **large / gigawatt-scale fleet** and licensed/proven tech + digital construction OS — don’t call them an SMR startup.

### Fuel cycle (optional depth)
Mine uranium → conversion → enrichment → fuel fabrication → reactor → used fuel storage/management. Specialized Direct supply chain; not this JD.

### Regulation
- **NRC** licenses reactors/designs in the US.
- Pathways jargon (Part 50 / 52) exists — **don’t volunteer** unless they go there; Part 52 is associated with standardized “design certification + combined license” thinking that rhymes with design-once-build-many. If unsure, say you’re still learning licensing pathways and stay at the business thesis level.

### Safety / Indirect touchpoints
You’re not the safety case owner. You still manage vendor risk, IT security, NDAs, insurance, and partner Legal on anything export-control-sensitive. JD **TRUST** = safety-culture language — intellectual honesty and accountability.

### Direct vs Indirect

| | Direct | Indirect (THIS ROLE) |
|---|--------|----------------------|
| What | Plant/product materials & construction packages | IT, professional services, marketing, workplace, contract labor, travel |
| Owner | Deployment / EPC / nuclear supply chain | **Sr. Category Manager Indirect/Corporate** |
| Your proof | Plenty hardware/BOM only if asked | Plenty multimillion $ indirect + Comcast enterprise tech/infra |
