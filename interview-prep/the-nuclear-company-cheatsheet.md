## Nuclear jargon (quick definitions)

| Term | Plain English |
|------|----------------|
| **Baseload** | Power that’s on almost all the time — nuclear’s product pitch |
| **Capacity factor** | How often a plant runs near full output; nuclear is typically among the highest |
| **Fission** | Splitting atoms in fuel → heat → (eventually) electricity |
| **PWR** | Pressurized Water Reactor — most common US type; primary loop stays liquid under pressure; secondary loop makes steam |
| **BWR** | Boiling Water Reactor — steam made in the reactor vessel itself; know it exists, lead with PWR |
| **FOAK** | First-of-a-kind — first build of a design; expensive learning (Vogtle 3/4 often cited as recent US example — finished but late/over budget) |
| **NOAK** | Nth-of-a-kind — later copies get cheaper/faster *if* you don’t reinvent |
| **Fleet-scale / design-once-build-many** | TNC thesis: standardize and repeat |
| **SMR** | Small modular reactor — different model (often hyperscaler-backed). TNC’s *public* pitch is large/gigawatt fleet, not “we’re an SMR startup” |
| **NRC** | US Nuclear Regulatory Commission — the regulator |
| **NQA-1** | Nuclear quality standard (shows up in Nucor/steel context) |

**PWR in one breath:** Fuel heats pressurized water that doesn’t boil → heat makes steam in a separate loop → steam spins the turbine.

**Fuel cycle (only if asked):** uranium mined → converted → enriched → fabricated into fuel → loaded in reactor → used fuel stored. Refuel many LWRs every ~18–24 months. Not your job.
