## Nuclear 101 — expanded (rehearse out loud)

### Why nuclear again (the “why now”)
1. **Baseload / firm power** — plants run near full output most of the year (**high capacity factor** — nuclear is typically the highest among major sources). Grid needs power that isn’t weather-dependent.
2. **Clean at the stack** — no CO₂ while generating; climate + corporate net-zero buyers care.
3. **Density** — lots of energy from a small land footprint vs equivalent *continuous* renewable + storage.
4. **Demand shock** — AI/data centers + electrification; US electricity demand forecasts jumped after years of flat load.
5. **Geopolitics** — Webb’s public line: China builds reactors at pace; US built almost none recently — energy = industrial + national security.

### How a plant makes electricity (any thermal plant, nuclear heat source)
**Fission** in fuel heats water → steam → **turbine** → **generator** → grid. Control rods / coolant systems manage the reaction. You’re not expected to derive neutron equations.

### PWR vs BWR (know the difference — their guide focuses on PWR)

| | **PWR** (Pressurized Water — know this) | **BWR** (Boiling Water — awareness) |
|---|------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------|
| Steam | Heat stays in **primary loop** (pressurized so it doesn’t boil) → **steam generator** heats a **secondary** loop that makes steam | Water **boils in the reactor vessel**; steam goes **directly** to turbine |
| Why PWR common | Most common US commercial type; secondary loop keeps radioactive primary water away from turbine | Fewer units; different ops profile |
| TNC angle | Standardized **PWR** designs repeated across a **fleet** | Don’t lead with BWR unless they do |

**PWR 15-second version:**  
> Fuel heats pressurized water that doesn’t boil; that heat makes steam in a separate loop; steam spins the turbine.

### FOAK vs NOAK / fleet (this is the money thesis)
- **FOAK (first-of-a-kind):** first build of a design — learning costs, rework, immature suppliers. **Vogtle 3/4** (AP1000 in Georgia) are the recent US example — finished, but late/over budget; industry lesson = finish design before pouring concrete, mature supply chain, trained workforce.
- **NOAK (nth-of-a-kind):** later copies of the *same* design — learning curve, cheaper/faster *if* you don’t reinvent.
- **Fleet-scale / design-once-build-many** = industrializing NOAK on purpose. TNC’s pitch.

### SMR vs large reactors (don’t mix them up)
- **SMR** = small modular reactors — factory modules, smaller MW, different go-to-market (hyperscalers often back these: Kairos, X-energy, etc.).
- **TNC public pitch** = **gigawatt-scale / large fleet** sites, proven licensed designs, sites closer to permits — not “we’re inventing a new tiny reactor brand.” Respect both; don’t say TNC *is* an SMR company.

### Fuel cycle (one layer deeper — optional if conversation goes there)
- Fuel is **uranium** (enriched for most light-water reactors).
- Chain: mine → convert → enrich → fabricate fuel assemblies → load reactor → used fuel storage.
- Refueling typically every **~18–24 months** for many LWRs — then back to high output.
- You don’t need enrichment physics; just know fuel is a specialized supply chain (Direct category elsewhere).

### Regulation / safety (vocabulary, not bravado)
- **NRC** = US Nuclear Regulatory Commission — licenses plants/designs.
- Safety culture is non-negotiable industry-wide; JD frames **TRUST** as safety culture language.
- Indirect still touches **vendor risk, cybersecurity for IT vendors, NDAs, insurance, export-control-aware suppliers** — partner Legal/Compliance; you’re not the licensing engineer.
