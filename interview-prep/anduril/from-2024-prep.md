## NPI / EVT–DVT–PVT (cheat sheet, not a speech)

Say it plain if they go manufacturing/NPI:

| Gate | Point |
|------|--------|
| **EVT** (engineering validation) | Does the design work? Prototype / lab pain. Specs still move. |
| **DVT** (design validation) | Can we build it the production way? Process + quality show up. |
| **PVT** (production validation) | Can the whole chain run at rate? Supply chain either holds or breaks. |

**Buyer/planner angle:**
> Early gates I’m hedging long leads and not freezing the wrong finish. Later gates I’m chasing dual source, quality escapes, and whether MRP matches the real BOM.

Form / fit / function = when a part change actually matters for interchangeability. Use only if they go there.

## Answers in human speech (rewrites of your 2024 Q&A)

### Tell me about yourself
> I’m Harrison. Most of my career is buying. Big chunk was Plenty — indoor farming, real manufacturing. Started on indirect, stood up Coupa and a bunch of systems, then moved to direct materials. That’s where I got into BOMs, NetSuite MRP, safety stock, packaging, nutrients, working with engineers on new cultivars and equipment. Cut a lot of BOM cost, lived a packaging recall, did the ugly planning work myself. I’m at Comcast now on big network categories. It’s fine. I want the factory seat again. Stephen and I talked about Copperhead and Seabed Sentry. That’s why I’m here.

### Why leave / why now
> Comcast is big-company category work. I already know how to do that. I miss owning materials on a line — shortages, long leads, engineering changes, quality escapes. This role is that job.

### No engineering degree
> Yeah, I’m not an ME. At Plenty I still sat with engineers and scientists on specs, SOWs, tooling, and new cultivars. Cold plasma equipment, hydrogel, packaging tools, spares BOMs. I don’t pretend I can design the part. I can learn the requirement fast, translate it into a buy, and keep the line fed when the drawing moves.

### Why Anduril
> They’re actually building hardware. Maritime in Quincy, vehicles that have to work in water. I like hard supply problems. I’ve already lived bad material lots and long-lead chaos at Plenty. Defense doesn’t freak me out. I know Stephen. The work fits.

### How do you negotiate / run suppliers
> I don’t try to “win” the meeting. I know the market, I know my BATNA, and I’m clear on what good looks like — price, lead time, quality, flexibility. Regular scorecards. When something breaks, I want a SCAR and a real CAPA, not a vibe. Relationships matter more when you don’t have DPAS leverage and you’re fighting for the same scarce parts as primes.

### How do you pick a supplier
> Start from the requirement. Can they actually make it? Capacity, quality system, money, past performance. RFQ with real specs. Then cost, lead time, risk, and whether they’ll work with engineering when the drawing changes. Site visit if it’s critical. Dual source if the part can kill the schedule. On maritime stuff I’d also ask about export / rating / who else is eating their capacity.

### Strength
> I learn systems fast. At Plenty I became the NetSuite reporting guy the Supply Chain Director pulled into everything. Same with Coupa. I’m useful when the data’s a mess and someone needs an answer today.

### Weakness
> Big-room presenting used to be rough. Supplier QBRs and internal teach-backs forced reps. Still not my favorite thing. I’d rather solve the shortage. I’m better than I was.

### Biggest win
> Coupa in about seven weeks, ~15 punchouts, policy that stuck — or the ~30% BOM cost takeout on directs. Pick one based on the room. Materials room = BOM. Process room = Coupa.

### Hardest challenge
> Packaging plastic cut (clamshell to tub, ~40%), seed FIFO in the warehouse, COVID scarce supply, or the sealer bottleneck — pick one and go deep. Don’t list all four.

### Disagreed with a decision
> Lab testing looked expensive. I ran internal vs external cost. Brought data. We changed the path. Disagreeing’s fine if you bring numbers.

### Mistake
> Double-ordered a cheap PO because it wasn’t marked ordered. Built filters so that couldn’t hide in the queue again. Low dollar. Process mattered more than the PO.

### Work style
> Mix. I like owning a book and not needing a meeting to move a PO. Also fine locked in a room with engineering and quality when a lot fails.

### Salary
> I want to understand the seat first. If it’s the right fit we’ll get to a number that works for both sides.

## Manufacturing-flavored prompts (short answers)

**Working with engineering**
> Regular sit-downs on specs and lead times. At Plenty that was new cultivars, tooling, cold plasma, spares. I bring supplier constraints early so we don’t design into a 30-week fantasy.

**High-mix / low-volume**
> That was Plenty. Couldn’t hit MOQs. We used forecast structures, restocking fees or roll-forward volume, and stocking on the nasty nutrients. Same problem Stephen flagged for low-volume parts here.

**Quality in manufacturing**
> Tub packaging went brittle and became a recall. Quarantine, good material in, vendor 100%, process review. That’s CAPA in real life.

**Lean / continuous improvement**
> No belt. I do bottleneck and waste for real. Sealer was the postharvest constraint — materials and tooling to raise throughput. Nutrient premix cut changeover ~20%.
