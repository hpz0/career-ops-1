# NorthStar business case — start here

## What this interview is (plain English)

Ramp is testing whether you can do the **Procurement Activation** job — help a mid-market customer go live on Ramp for purchasing.

It’s **two parts:**

| Part | Time | What happens |
|------|------|----------------|
| **Part 1 — Design session** | ~15 min | You play **Harrison**, Activation specialist. **Monica** is the Controller at fake customer **NorthStar Aviation** (NetSuite, already on Ramp for expense). You walk through your deck, recommend how **aircraft parts** buying should work, ask smart questions, and **book a next step** before you hang up. |
| **Part 2 — Triage** | ~10 min | They give you **4 made-up client situations**. You **rank** them 1→4 by urgency and say **what you’d do next** on each. You’re managing a book of accounts — protect the **60-day activation window** and **first live purchase request**. |

You are **not** being tested on memorizing Ramp feature names. You **are** being tested on: procurement judgment, listening, prescribing a sensible path, and closing with owners + calendar.

## The one story to hold in your head

**NorthStar buys aircraft parts.** Approvals are slow (parts miss **need-by dates**). There’s no clean **trail from request to payment** — it’s in email, Slack, spreadsheets.

**Your recommendation:** Stand up **one parts buying path** first (not every category). Request → approve → PO in NetSuite → receive → match invoice → pay. **Pilot** with a small group. Run **one real request** this week. **Lock a working session** on the calendar before you leave.

**Your wow moment:** Don’t assume Inventory must approve. Ask: is that step for **visibility** (do they lack stock info?) or **accountability** (someone must own the decision)? — then design around her answer.
