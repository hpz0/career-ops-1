## Abigail's questions → better answers

### "You're a CFO with no procurement — why does procurement matter?"

> Procurement is how spend becomes **controlled and visible** before it hits the card or AP. Without it you get maverick buying, duplicate vendors, no contract coverage, and finance finds out at month-end. Good procurement gives you **approved vendors, PO discipline, and audit trail** — and it frees the CFO to forecast instead of firefight. At Plenty we went from wild-west purchasing to **99% on contract** — that's the difference between chaos and a finance function that can scale.

### "Do you manage a team at Comcast?"

**Honest + manager frame:**

> I don't have direct reports at Comcast — I'm in a **senior individual contributor** sourcing role on multi-billion categories. But I **coach and mentor peers** on contract structure, negotiation strategy, and stakeholder alignment. Before that at Plenty I **managed three buyers** and built the function. For this role I'd lead the same way: **clear standards, shadowing, QA on implementations**, and hiring ICs as volume grows.

### "How have you used AI in your job?"

**Honest — don't oversell:**

> I'm an **early adopter**, not a data scientist. I use AI for **drafting**, **summarizing long contracts/RFPs**, and **structuring analysis** — always verified before anything operational. In procurement specifically I'd use it for **spend classification, exception detection, and contract metadata** — the same problems Ramp is building agents for. I won't let AI approve spend; I **will** use it to give finance visibility faster.
