## Framing that landed (keep)

The job is asking: *Can you create the procurement center of excellence for the implementation org?* Founding manager = **thinking + systems** over “I managed N people for Y years.” No playbook to inherit — you write it.

## 1. How would you build this team from scratch?

**Answer (speakable):**
> I’d define outcomes before headcount. The team’s job isn’t installing software — it’s getting mid-market customers to adopt better procurement processes quickly.
>
> Early on I’d organize around **complexity**, not industry: specialists for standard mid-market motions, stronger ICs for messy ERP/approval/legal deals, and me as escalation + Product feedback loop. Once volume justifies it, specialize further (onboarding vs optimization vs integration-heavy). Early flexibility beats premature specialization.

**Your proof:** S6 coaching model; Plenty team of 3; playbook plan hiring scorecard.

---

## 2. How would you justify adding headcount?

**Don’t say:** “When everyone is busy.”

**Answer:**
> I’d use a capacity model: implementation backlog, median time-to-go-live, utilization, CSAT/escalations after go-live. If specialists are ~80% utilized *and* cycle times or quality are slipping, the **system** is constrained — not a grit problem. I’d show when one more hire creates more customer value than delaying.

**Your metrics to name:** time-to-go-live · on-time % · specialist independence · escalation/reopen rate.

## 3. How would you build the implementation playbook?

Merge ChatGPT’s “four layers” with your stage model:

| Layer | What you say | Your detail |
|-------|--------------|-------------|
| **Discovery** | Current process, stakeholders, approvals, ERP, AP, pain | Refresher §§2–4; leave with current-state map |
| **Design** | Current → future-on-Ramp → config decisions → success criteria | Explicit “won’t do in v1” |
| **Implementation** | Repeatable checklist (not every deal reinvented) | Discovery → config → education → go-live (+ weeks as a *template*, not dogma) |
| **Continuous improvement** | Every deal: what surprised us / took longest / confused them / should become standard → update playbook | Red deal → playbook edit or Product ticket |

**Week template (flexible, ChatGPT-inspired):** W1 discovery/alignment → W2 config → W3 test → W4 train → W5 go-live — stretch only when complexity requires.

## 4. How would you develop your people?

**Answer:**
> Good managers reduce dependency on themselves. Clear expectations, shadow then reverse-shadow on customer calls, structured feedback after implementations, scorecards, and 1:1s on **skill/judgment** — not status theater. Ownership increases as quality holds.

**Your proof:** S6 (junior buyer → IT category owner). Don’t apologize for “only 3 people” — quality of coaching > headcount.

---

## 5. What KPIs would you track?

Keep it short in interview (pick 5–6). Full menu from share + our plan:

| Bucket | KPIs |
|--------|------|
| **Operational** | Time-to-go-live, on-time go-live %, first-pass / reopen rate, config rework |
| **Customer** | Time-to-first-value, post-impl CSAT, adoption of intake/approvals (not just logins) |
| **Team** | Utilization, new-hire ramp time, escalation rate to manager, playbook adherence |
| **Business** | Procurement volume activated, feature adoption, expansion readiness / handoff quality |

**Say three first:** time-to-go-live · on-time rate · specialist independence.

## 6. How would you work with Product?

**Answer:**
> Every implementation teaches Product something. I’d bucket feedback: bugs · missing functionality · UX friction · feature requests — then bring **recurring themes with frequency and time-to-go-live impact**, not a firehose of one-off asks.

**Your seed example:** S7 credit-packet / term-safe vendor onboarding (if Product talks automation of credit apps).

## 8. How do you balance speed with quality?

**Answer (Ramp-native):**
> I’d optimize for **repeatability**, not perfection. Early process should be good enough to create consistency. Then iterate from operational data and customer friction — don’t wait for the perfect playbook before shipping v1.

Ties to: ugly-and-usable playbook; Coupa lesson that policy must ship with the tool.

## 9. “Tell me about something you built from scratch”

**Use Plenty — not Comcast.**

**Structure:**
- **Situation:** Fragmented buying / need for company-wide P2P discipline at Plenty  
- **Why old way failed:** Manual/maverick paths; no shared operating rules  
- **What you built:** Coupa P2P + Procurement Policy + operating model; CLM (Ironclad/DocuSign); coached team to run it  
- **Stakeholders:** Finance, Legal, IT, buyers  
- **Resistance:** Process change / adoption (speak honestly if asked; don’t invent drama)  
- **Metrics:** 99% on contract; req cycle 3 → 1.5 days; e-invoice +50%  
- **Learning:** Platform only works when policy + behavior move with it — same spine I’d use for Ramp’s activation playbook  

Comcast = optional *complexity/stakeholder* color, not the “built the system” story.
