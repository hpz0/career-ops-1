## Nuclear Energy 101 (for someone who knows nothing)

Memorize the *plain English* versions. Recruiter doesn’t need a physics lecture — she needs you not to freeze if “PWR” or “baseload” comes up.

### Why nuclear exists as a product
1. **Baseload:** Nuclear plants run nearly 24/7 for years between refueling — firm power when the grid needs reliability.
2. **Clean (operating):** No CO₂ at the stack while generating; climate + AI/data-center demand narrative is why capital is flowing back in.
3. **Dense:** Huge energy from a small footprint vs acres of solar for equivalent continuous output (rough comparison — don’t cite invented numbers).

### PWR (Pressurized Water Reactor) — their Interview Guide tip
- Most common US commercial reactor type.
- **How it works (3 sentences):** Nuclear fuel heats water in a **closed primary loop** under high pressure (so it doesn’t boil). That heat exchanges into a **secondary loop** that makes steam. Steam spins a **turbine → generator → electricity**.
- Why it matters to TNC’s pitch: **standardized PWR designs repeated across a fleet** = learning curve + cost-down (Interview Guide + company “design once, build many”).

### Fleet-scale (procurement translation)
- Building **one** nuclear plant historically = unique design, custom supply chain, overruns.
- Fleet-scale = **same playbook many times** — suppliers, contracts, specs, logistics can be standardized.
- **Your analogy (honest, from your CV):** At Plenty you built repeatable procurement (policy, Coupa, preferred suppliers, catalogs). At Comcast you run tech/infra categories at multi-entity scale. TNC needs that *corporate* muscle while the company scales offices, IT, professional services, and workplace around a massive deployment machine.

### Direct vs Indirect (say this correctly)
| | Direct | Indirect (THIS ROLE) |
|---|--------|----------------------|
| What | Materials that go into the product/plant (steel, components, fuel-related, construction packages) | Goods/services that run the *company*: **IT/SaaS, professional services, marketing, facilities/workplace, contract labor, travel** |
| Who owns at TNC | Likely deployment / EPC / supply-chain org (not this JD) | **You** — Sr. Category Manager Indirect/Corporate |
| Your proof | Plenty hardware/BOM adjacent story only if asked | Plenty multimillion $ **indirect** book + Comcast enterprise tech/infra sourcing |

### Safety / regulation one-liner (don’t overclaim)
- Nuclear is heavily regulated (NRC in the US). You’re not the licensing engineer. As indirect category manager you still care about **vendor risk, data security, export-control-adjacent vendors, insurance, NDAs** — partner Legal/Compliance; don’t invent nuclear QA expertise.

### Honest gap script (if asked “do you know nuclear?”)
> I don’t come from a nuclear utility background — and this seat is Indirect/Corporate, not safety-class materials. I’ve done the homework on why fleet-scale and design-once-build-many matter, and how PWRs fit the US fleet story. What I bring day one is category strategy for IT, professional services, and workplace spend — standing up scalable purchasing while the company grows. I’m a fast learner on domain vocabulary; I won’t fake reactor physics.
